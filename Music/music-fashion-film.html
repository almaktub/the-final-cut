Overall Score
"A concept album about whether the persona and the person underneath it were ever actually different things, that trades Brat's immediacy for something colder and funnier, and loses real heat in the trade."
7.9 because the cover tells you everything before track one starts: Scorsese, John Cale, Marc Jacobs, and no Charli. She isn't claiming greatness, she's interrogating whether she's earned it, and that's a genuinely interesting hook for a victory-lap album to refuse. Persona, 2007, and the Cronenberg-featuring closer are where the idea and the songwriting fully line up, and they're as good as anything she's made. The 2.1: a 30-minute, 11-track runtime doesn't leave room for the weaker premises (Card Declined, SS26) to become more than their premise, and the constant push between irony and earnestness occasionally curdles into just not trusting the record. That split is real. Some of the same tracks that got Pitchfork and the Guardian handing out top marks left the Times and the Telegraph cold.

Persona vs. Self
The war between the image she built and the person underneath it, played out most directly on "Persona," where she stops fighting the persona and fuses with it on the outro instead.
🎸
Genre as Costume
"Rock Music" turns her own "I think the dance floor is dead" quote into thesis and troll at once. She isn't committing to rock, she's performing the idea of it, and the glitch on the title line is the tell.
📼
Nostalgia as Trap
"2007" sets 2000s indie-landfill alt-rock against modern TikTok fame, and fractures its own chorus into DJ-scratch static right when the contradiction lands.
⏳
Mortality and Legacy
"No One Lasts Forever" hands the mic to David Cronenberg for a meditation on art outliving the artist, his voice looped and doubled against itself so nothing quite syncs up.

Track by Track
The Dissection
01Rock Music
7.9▼
The thesis statement dressed as a joke. Her own "the dance floor is dead" quote from the rollout becomes the song's premise and its own punchline in the same breath. She isn't committing to rock, she's performing the idea of it, and the glitch on "ro-o-o-ock music" is a playful enough tell that even the trick feels human.
02SS26
7.4▼
Named for a fashion season, sung like the season already ended. Fame reads here as a runway you can't get off. Catchy in the verses, hollow in the chorus on purpose, which is the point and also the album's biggest risk.
03Card Declined
6.6▼
The most style-over-substance moment on the record. The refrain piles up commands and details until it becomes a collage, meant to dramatize hiding behind consumption. The idea lands before the song does, and three and a half minutes is long for a joke this thin.
04Camera
7.7▼
She claims film, more than music, is where she can finally be honest, in a song, which is either a wink or a contradiction depending on your patience for her. Worth it for the tension alone, and a sign the record knows exactly what it's doing.
052007
8.3▼
The best trick on the album. Alt-rock guitars built to sound like the year the title names, chorus fractured into DJ-scratch static right when the TikTok-fame lyric lands, so nostalgia and the thing that killed it happen in the same four bars.
06I'm Afraid
7.0▼
The plainest song on the record and one of the more effective ones for it. Less armor than almost anything else here, which after ten tracks of irony actually lands as relief rather than weakness.
07Yeah
7.3▼
Barely a song. Two lines total, "I don't wanna" answered by "wait, I think I wanna feel it," repeated until the chorus stops and starts like it's losing signal. Feels like a first draft on purpose, and mostly earns the choice as a moshpit starter.
08Wink Wink
8.5▼
The funniest song on the album and maybe the sharpest. The one track where the joke about fame and the actual feeling about fame are allowed to sit in the same line without either one undercutting the other.
09Persona
8.9▼
The record's actual center. "Don't fucking choke on your ego when you see me coming, I know you're performing, I know that you're hurting" is the plainest she gets about the cost of the act, and the outro, where she fuses with the persona instead of fighting it, is the smartest writing on the album.
10Magic Metal Montana
8.7▼
The one moment with no argument attached. A love letter to A.G. Cook and to collaboration itself, harmonized chorus floating over sugar-sweet guitar. After nine tracks of self-interrogation, letting one song just be happy is its own kind of relief.
11No One Lasts Forever
9.0▼
David Cronenberg reciting a meditation on mortality while his own voice loops back against itself, one line landing just behind the other, so nothing quite lines up. "Oblivion is freedom," he says, and she doesn't argue. The best closing track she's made.

Footnote
The cover has no Charli on it. Scorsese, Cale, and Jacobs earn that photo because their legacies are already settled, hers isn't, and the whole album is what that uncertainty sounds like. She doesn't get to assume the work survives. She only gets the sound of trying to figure out if it will while the tape still rolls.
